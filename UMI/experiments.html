Further research directions

These extensions need their own implementations and validation.

on the horizon

A model, or a person

The interface never assumes the subject is a model. Present the same input on a screen and record the person's behavior (or, with electrodes, their neural responses), and a person meets the same contract — so a test can, in principle, run on either.

on the horizon

Reading and writing to the brain

Recording is one direction; stimulation is the other. The same machinery that reads a region can drive it — the basis for testing decoders and sensory implants.

on the horizon

More than one subject at once

Several subjects can share one session, one's output becoming another's input — the opening for studying interaction between two models, or between a model and a person.

on the horizon

Body signals, not just choices

Behavior isn't only button presses. Pupil size and skin response are readouts on their own channels, so physiological measures sit beside the neural and behavioral ones.

Finding a model's word-selective units

To study reading, we first have to find the model's word-selective units. We do it the way brain scientists do: show the model words and non-words, and keep the neurons that fire much harder for real words.

Gathering them from wherever they live

The usual approach reads from a single layer of the model; here, instead, we collect the word-selective units from across several layers and treat them as one group, because that's where they naturally turn out to be.
